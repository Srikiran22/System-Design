# 78 — Standards, regulation and certification: deep assurance map

## Standards are scoped instruments, not universal checklists

A standard's title alone does not establish applicability. Determine: system type; intended use; hazard/consequence; jurisdiction; regulator; contract; edition/amendments; certification basis; and whether the document is normative, guidance, profile or informative material. Record the legal/contractual force separately from technical relevance.

## Safety and security families

- IEC 61508 is the general functional-safety foundation for E/E/PE safety-related systems [S131].
- IEC 61511 addresses process-industry safety instrumented systems.
- ISO 26262 addresses functional safety of road-vehicle E/E systems; ISO 21448 addresses safety of intended functionality (SOTIF).
- Aerospace development and assurance commonly involve SAE ARP4754B, ARP4761A, RTCA DO-178C, DO-254 and DO-160; actual obligations depend on aircraft/system certification basis and regulator policy.
- Medical-device risk/software lifecycle commonly involves ISO 14971 and IEC 62304, along with device classification and jurisdictional regulatory controls.
- Rail lifecycle/software/safety references include EN 50126, EN 50128 and EN 50129.
- Machinery control safety includes IEC 62061 and ISO 13849.
- Nuclear I&C safety and regulatory assurance requires nuclear-specific standards, regulator expectations and safety-case evidence.
- Industrial control security uses IEC 62443 alongside functional safety; the two assurance disciplines must be coordinated.

## Cybersecurity guidance

NIST SP 800-160 is systems security/cyber-resiliency engineering, SP 800-207 is zero-trust architecture, SP 800-218 is secure software development and SP 800-161 is cybersecurity supply-chain risk management [S129, S211, S212, S213, S24]. NIST CSF 2.0 is a risk-management framework, not a complete technical design specification. AI RMF/GenAI profiles add AI lifecycle risk activities but do not replace sector safety or privacy requirements.

## Regulatory assurance chain

A robust compliance/evidence plan links:

1. Applicable authority and governing requirement.
2. System classification, boundary and intended use.
3. Compliance obligation and responsible owner.
4. Requirement/control and allocated design element.
5. Supplier/development process evidence.
6. Verification/validation method and result.
7. Configuration and change impact.
8. Residual risk, exception and acceptance authority.
9. Post-deployment monitoring and incident reporting obligations.

## Credential vs approval

A professional credential demonstrates an individual's assessed knowledge/experience under a credentialing scheme. It does not approve a system, certify a design, authorize operation or replace regulator/authority review. Likewise, completing a training course does not establish compliance.

## Publication warning

Normative standard texts can be licensed, and their editions may be amended or superseded. Verify current status directly with the standards owner, regulator and contractual authority before using this map for compliance, procurement or certification.
