# 26 — Systemantics, human judgment and adjacent thinking books

These works can improve judgment and communication, but they are not substitutes for engineering standards, system dynamics methods, architecture evaluation or empirical evidence. The website should label them as **complementary reading**, not normative technical references.

## Thinking in Systems — Donella H. Meadows

Use for: stocks/flows, feedback, delay, nonlinear behavior, system boundaries, mental models, leverage points and archetypes. Best paired with behavior-over-time graphs, causal-loop diagrams, stock-flow models and a real process-improvement exercise.

Primary project resources: https://donellameadows.org/systems-thinking-resources/

## Systemantics / The Systems Bible — John Gall

Use for: skeptical reflection on over-designed systems, emergent side effects, system growth, complexity and the danger of assuming that large systems behave as their designers intend. The work is satirical and aphoristic. Treat its “laws” as prompts for critique and hypothesis generation, not validated physical laws or engineering acceptance criteria.

Bibliographic starting point: https://books.google.com/books/about/Systemantics.html?hl=en&id=8dkaAAAAMAAJ

## The Basic Laws of Human Stupidity — Carlo M. Cipolla

Use for: thinking about human behavior, organizational externalities and decisions whose costs/benefits are misperceived. It is a social/philosophical essay, not a predictive behavioral model or engineering method. Do not use it to label stakeholders or dismiss dissent.

## The Four Agreements — Don Miguel Ruiz

Use only as optional personal reflection on communication, assumptions and interpersonal conduct. It is a self-help/spiritual book, not a systems engineering or systems thinking textbook. Its themes can be translated carefully into collaboration habits: communicate clearly, verify assumptions, avoid attributing intent without evidence, and make an honest effort. Keep it in a separate “communication and personal development” shelf.

Official author page: https://www.miguelruiz.com/the-four-agreements/

## Brian Douglas — control systems learning

Use as an approachable visual learning supplement for feedback control, PID, stability, state-space and control intuition. Pair video explanations with mathematical derivation, simulation, lab evidence and formal domain references. YouTube channel: https://www.youtube.com/@BrianBDouglas

## Reading sequence recommendation

1. Meadows for systems foundations.
2. SEBoK/NASA for formal systems engineering.
3. Gall as a critical-thinking companion after learners understand basic methods.
4. Brian Douglas alongside a control-systems curriculum.
5. Cipolla and Ruiz only as optional general human/communication reflection.

The site should clearly separate technical authority, practitioner experience, critical-thinking essays and personal-development material.
